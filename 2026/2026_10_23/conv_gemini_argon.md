# Prompt 1
Given these two papers: 
 - **Hammoud et al. (2022)**: *["Smartphone-based ecological momentary assessment reveals mental health benefits of birdlife"](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-022-20207-6)*
 - **Stobbe et al. (2022)**: *["Birdsongs alleviate anxiety and paranoia in healthy participants"](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-022-20841-0)*

Could you have a go at design a protocol or experiment with the following properties?
 1. It would suit the average busy adult (non-scientist) human living in an urban environment
 2. It wouldn't require more than 5 minutes a day. If there are days when nothing is to be done, the 5 minutes allocated for that day could be used on another day.
 3. It wouldn't require expensive technology or equipment: a smartphone, pen and paper would be ideal.
 4. It would establish a kind of baseline so that the person doing the experiment (on themselves) could see actual results (or the absence of results) and be able to demonstrate them or tell the story to their friends.
 5. Hopefully the protocol could be able to demonstrate results or the absence of results in a period of 1 to 3 months.
 6. It should be hopefully fun and enjoyable... not a drag or tough to perform the protocol.

Thanks!

# Response 1 (no url context)
Here is a practical, engaging self-experimentation protocol—an **"N-of-1" trial**—that bridges the real-world observational method of *Hammoud et al.* (who tracked random daily moments via smartphone) with the controlled acoustic interventions of *Stobbe et al.* (who tested recorded high-diversity birdsong vs. urban traffic noise).

To make it fun, easy to stick to, and visually shareable with friends, we will call it **The 6-Week "Urban Tweeter" Challenge**.

---

### 🛠 The Toolkit (Zero Cost)
1. **A Pocket Notebook & Pen** (Or a very basic notes/spreadsheet app on your phone). *Bonus: Get two colored highlighters (e.g., Green and Grey).*
2. **A Smartphone & Earphones.**
3. **A "High-Diversity" Birdsong Playlist:** Before starting, spend 5 minutes finding a 3-to-5-minute audio track of a rich dawn chorus (containing multiple bird species, as *Stobbe et al.* found high species diversity works best).
4. **The Cornell Lab "Merlin Bird ID" App (Free):** This is the "secret sauce" to make the experiment fun rather than a chore. Its *Sound ID* feature listens to the environment and highlights in real-time what birds are singing around you—even in the middle of a noisy city.

---

### 📋 The Daily Micro-Scorecard (Takes 15 seconds)
Instead of long scientific questionnaires, you will rate two metrics on a simple **1 to 10 scale** whenever prompted:
* **Calm Score (Anxiety):** *1 = Completely stressed/on edge | 10 = Totally zen and relaxed.*
* **Vibe Score (Mood & Social Trust/Paranoia):** *1 = Gloomy, irritable, suspicious of people around me | 10 = Cheerful, open, feeling good about the world.*

---

### 📅 The 6-Week Protocol (42 Days)
To keep it well under the 5-minute daily limit, you only do the protocol **5 days a week (e.g., Monday–Friday)**. Weekends are completely off! You can bank your unused weekend minutes for the final analysis.

#### Phase 1: The Urban Baseline (Weeks 1 & 2)
*Inspired by Hammoud et al.’s Ecological Momentary Assessment (EMA).*
* **The Setup:** Set 2 random alarms on your phone during your waking hours (e.g., 10:30 AM and 3:15 PM). 
* **The Action (1 minute total/day):** When the alarm goes off, pause for 10 seconds and notice your surroundings. Jot down in your notebook:
  1. Did you see or hear a bird right now? (**Yes / No**)
  2. What is the dominant sound? (**Traffic/City / Quiet / Nature**)
  3. Your **Calm Score** (1–10) and **Vibe Score** (1–10).
* *Goal:* This builds your personal baseline without changing your routine, showing how your normal urban environment affects your mood.

#### Phase 2: The Commuter’s Earphones (Weeks 3 & 4)
*Inspired by Stobbe et al.’s audio intervention.*
* **The Action (4 minutes/day):** Pick one consistent transition point in your day—like waiting for the subway, sitting on the bus, or taking a mid-afternoon coffee break.
  1. **Score Yourself (Before):** Write down your Calm & Vibe scores (10 seconds).
  2. **The A/B Test (3.5 minutes):** 
     * On **Mon / Wed / Fri**, put in your earphones and listen to your **Birdsong Track** for 3.5 minutes.
     * On **Tue / Thu**, take your earphones out and just listen to the **raw urban/office soundscape** for 3.5 minutes.
  3. **Score Yourself (After):** Write down your new Calm & Vibe scores (10 seconds).

#### Phase 3: The Urban Safari (Weeks 5 & 6)
*Combining both papers into an active, fun habit.*
* **The Action (4 minutes/day):** During your daily commute or lunch walk, open the **Merlin Bird ID app** and hit "Sound ID" for 4 minutes. 
  1. Score your **Before** Calm & Vibe (10 seconds).
  2. Walk slowly or stand near a patch of urban trees/bushes while watching the app identify the hidden birds around you in real-time (3.5 minutes). 
  3. Write down your **After** Calm & Vibe scores, plus the **number of bird species** the app detected (20 seconds).

---

### 📊 Telling the Story (Using your "Banked" Time)
At the end of the 6 weeks, take 20 minutes (using the time you saved on your days off) to crunch your numbers and create three fun, shareable takeaways for your friends:

1. **The "Hammoud Effect" (From Phase 1):** 
   Highlight all the Phase 1 days where you accidentally heard/saw a bird in **Green**, and the traffic-only days in **Grey**. Calculate your average Calm & Vibe score for Green vs. Grey. 
   * *The Story:* *"When I randomly noticed birds in the city, my mood was X% higher than when I only heard traffic."*
2. **The "Stobbe Delta" (From Phase 2):** 
   Calculate the average *change* (After score minus Before score) for your Earphone Birdsong days versus your City Noise days.
   * *The Story:* *"Listening to 3 minutes of recorded birdsong on the subway dropped my stress by X points, whereas listening to the city noise actually made me more tense."*
3. **The "Pokédex" Bonus (From Phase 3):** 
   Count how many unique urban bird species you "collected" in the Merlin app over Weeks 5 & 6, and compare your Phase 3 average mood to your Phase 1 baseline.
   * *The Story:* *"I had no idea there were 14 different species of birds living on my walk to work—and actively hunting for their songs for 4 minutes a day boosted my overall baseline mood from a 5.5 to a 7.8!"*

### Why this works for a busy urbanite:
* **No friction:** Phase 1 takes 60 seconds a day; Phases 2 and 3 piggypack onto dead time (commuting or walking to get lunch).
* **Built-in Control Group:** By alternating days in Phase 2 and taking a 2-week baseline in Phase 1, you protect yourself against the placebo effect and bad-weather/bad-work days.
* **Gamification:** Using acoustic recognition in Phase 3 turns a mental-health protocol into a real-life treasure hunt.

# Response 1 (url context on)
Here is a customized, self-experimentation protocol (often called an **$N=1$ study**) designed specifically around the findings of **Hammoud et al. (2022)** (which looked at real-life encounters with birds via smartphone prompts) and **Stobbe et al. (2022)** (which looked at how listening to diverse birdsong audio reduces anxiety/paranoia compared to urban traffic noise).

Let's call it **"Project: Urban Chirp."** 

---

### 📋 Overview & Time Budget
* **Total Duration:** 8 Weeks (2 Months).
* **The Time Bank:** 5 minutes/day = **35 minutes per week**. 
* Rather than forcing a rigid daily schedule, this protocol uses a **"Time Banking" model**. Most days take **30 seconds**. A few days a week take **6 to 10 minutes**. One day at the very end takes **20 minutes** to crunch your numbers.

---

### 🛠️ Equipment Needed
1. **A Smartphone:** For alarms, a stopwatch, and a free bird-ID app (**Merlin Bird ID** by the Cornell Lab is free, fun, and acts like "Shazam for birds").
2. **Headphones:** Whatever you normally use with your phone.
3. **A small pocket notebook & pen** (or a simple Notes/Spreadsheet app on your phone).
4. **A 6-minute track of diverse birdsong:** Stobbe et al. found that *high diversity* (multiple species singing) works best, and used a 6-minute exposure. Find a track on Spotify/YouTube of a "dawn chorus" or "woodland birdsong" and save it offline.

---

### 📏 The "30-Second Check-In" (Your Measurement Tool)
In both papers, researchers measured momentary mental states. Whenever this protocol asks you to "Score Yourself," you will take 30 seconds to jot down three numbers from **1 (Not at all) to 10 (Extremely)** based on how you feel *right in that exact moment*:
1. **Mood / Cheerfulness:** (1 = gloomy, 10 = great) *(from Hammoud)*
2. **Calmness / Relaxation:** (1 = super anxious/tense, 10 = completely zen) *(from Stobbe)*
3. **Social Comfort:** (1 = suspicious/annoyed by people/paranoid, 10 = trusting/at ease with the world) *(from Stobbe)*

---

### 🗓️ The 8-Week Protocol

#### **Phase 1: The Urban Baseline (Weeks 1–2)**
*Goal: Establish your normal mental state in the city without changing your behavior.*
* **What to do:** Set **1 random alarm per day** on your phone during waking hours. 
* When it goes off, take **30 seconds** to record:
  1. Your 3 Check-In Scores (Mood, Calm, Social).
  2. **Bird Check:** Can you currently see or hear a bird right now? (**Yes / No**).
* *Time spent:* 30 seconds/day (3.5 mins/week). **You bank ~31 minutes a week** for later phases!

---

#### **Phase 2: The "Stobbe" Audio Experiment (Weeks 3–4)**
*Goal: Test whether 6 minutes of birdsong audio beats normal urban noise for reducing stress.*
* **What to do:** You only do this **4 days a week** (e.g., Mon, Tue, Thu, Fri), ideally during a commute, lunch break, or mid-afternoon slump.
  * **2 of the days (Control Days - e.g., Mon/Thu):** Score yourself (30s). Spend **6 minutes** sitting or walking while listening to the raw ambient city noise around you (traffic, construction, chatter—no music/podcasts). Score yourself again (30s).
  * **2 of the days (Bird Audio Days - e.g., Tue/Fri):** Score yourself (30s). Put on headphones and listen to your **6-minute diverse birdsong track**. Score yourself again (30s).
* *Time spent:* 7 minutes × 4 days = **28 minutes/week** (Under your 35-minute weekly budget!).

---

#### **Phase 3: The "Hammoud" Real-World Safari (Weeks 5–6)**
*Goal: Actively seek out real urban birds and make it a game.*
* **What to do:** **3 days a week**, use your banked time to go on a **10-minute "Micro-Safari"** in your neighborhood, a local park, or a tree-lined street. 
  1. Score yourself before you start (30s).
  2. Open the **Merlin Bird ID app** and hit "Sound ID." Walk slowly and watch the app light up in real-time with photos of the birds singing around you. Try to spot them in the trees or on rooftops. Write down your "species high score" for the day!
  3. Score yourself right after the 10 minutes (30s).
  4. **Bonus (Hammoud test):** 2 hours later, take 30 seconds to score yourself *again* (Hammoud et al. found that the mood boost from seeing/hearing birds lingers for hours!).
* *Time spent:* ~11.5 minutes × 3 days = **34.5 minutes/week**.

---

#### **Phase 4: The "Washout" & Final Week (Weeks 7–8)**
*Goal: See what happens when you stop forcing it, and crunch the data.*
* **Week 7 (Return to Baseline):** Go back to Phase 1 (just one 30-second check-in per day when the alarm goes off, noting Yes/No for birds). Does your awareness of birds stay higher than in Week 1?
* **Week 8 (Analysis Week):** Do nothing for 6 days! Spend your banked 20–30 minutes at a café on the weekend calculating your results.

---

### 📊 How to Crunch the Numbers & Tell the Story to Friends

Take your notebook, grab a calculator, and calculate **4 simple averages** (you can adding Mood + Calm + Social together for a "Total Wellbeing Score out of 30", or look at them individually):

1. **The "Passive Encounter" Effect (Weeks 1 & 7):** 
   * Compare your average score on random alarms where Bird = **YES** vs. Bird = **NO**. 
   * *The Story:* *"Even when I wasn't trying, on days I happened to hear a pigeon or a sparrow when my alarm went off, my mood was X% higher."*
2. **The "Headphone Commute" Effect (Weeks 3 & 4):**
   * Compare the *change in score* (After minus Before) on **City Noise Days** vs. **Birdsong Audio Days**.
   * *The Story:* *"Listening to 6 minutes of woodland birds on the subway dropped my stress score by X points, whereas listening to city traffic actually made me more tense."*
3. **The "Pokemon GO for Birds" Effect (Weeks 5 & 6):**
   * Look at the *change in score* before vs. after your 10-minute Merlin Bird ID walks—and whether the score stayed high 2 hours later.
   * *The Story:* *"I identified 14 different bird species living on my block that I never knew existed, and a 10-minute bird walk boosted my mood for the entire afternoon."*
4. **The "Noticeability" Shift (Week 1 vs. Week 7):**
   * Count how many "Bird = YES" entries you had in Week 1 vs. Week 7. 
   * *The Story:* Very often, the birds were always there, but by Week 7 your brain has tuned out the traffic and tuned *into* the birds!

### Why this works for a busy human:
* **No daily grind:** Only Weeks 1 and 7 require daily interaction, and it takes 30 seconds.
* **Built-in gamification:** Using Sound ID in the Merlin app feels like collecting Pokémon in real life—it turns gray urban streets into a scavenger hunt.
* **Built-in scientific controls:** Because you have a baseline (Phase 1), an active control (City noise in Phase 2), and a washout (Phase 4), your friends (even the skeptical ones) will have to respect your data!
